# References: International Trade

1. [Comparative Advantage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparative_advantage) - Wikipedia - Explains David Ricardo's foundational insight that countries benefit from trade by specializing in goods they produce at the lowest opportunity cost.

2. [Balance of Trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balance_of_trade) - Wikipedia - Covers trade deficits and surpluses, how they are measured, what they mean for an economy, and common misconceptions about trade balances.

3. [Tariff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tariff) - Wikipedia - Describes how taxes on imports affect prices, domestic producers, consumers, and government revenue, including historical and modern examples of trade policy.

4. International Economics: Theory and Policy (12th Edition) - Paul Krugman, Maurice Obstfeld, Marc Melitz - Pearson - Leading textbook on international trade theory, trade policy, exchange rates, and the global financial system by a Nobel Prize winner.

5. The Travels of a T-Shirt in the Global Economy (2nd Edition) - Pietra Rivoli - Wiley - Engaging narrative that traces a t-shirt through the global supply chain, illustrating comparative advantage, trade policy, and globalization in action.

6. [Free Trade](https://www.econlib.org/library/Enc/FreeTrade.html) - Concise Encyclopedia of Economics - Expert discussion of why economists overwhelmingly support free trade, the gains from specialization, and the costs of protectionism.

7. [Protectionism](https://www.econlib.org/library/Enc/Protectionism.html) - Concise Encyclopedia of Economics - Analyzes tariffs, quotas, and other trade barriers, explaining who benefits and who loses from protectionist policies.

8. [U.S. Trade Balance](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/BOPGSTB) - Federal Reserve Economic Data (FRED) - Monthly data on the difference between U.S. exports and imports, showing the trade deficit trend and its relationship to the broader economy.

9. [U.S. Dollar Exchange Rate](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DTWEXBGS) - Federal Reserve Economic Data (FRED) - Trade-weighted dollar index showing how the value of the U.S. dollar changes relative to other currencies, affecting import and export prices.

10. [World Trade Data](https://data.worldbank.org/indicator/NE.TRD.GNFS.ZS) - World Bank - International data on trade as a percentage of GDP across countries, illustrating how globalized different economies are and trends in world trade.
