# References: Employment and Unemployment

1. [Unemployment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unemployment) - Wikipedia - Comprehensive overview of unemployment including how it is measured, the different types (frictional, structural, cyclical), and its economic and social costs.

2. [Labor Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_force) - Wikipedia - Explains who counts as part of the labor force, how participation rates are calculated, and trends in labor force composition over time.

3. [Human Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_capital) - Wikipedia - Covers the economic concept of skills, education, and training as a form of capital that increases worker productivity and earning potential.

4. Macroeconomics (11th Edition) - N. Gregory Mankiw - Worth Publishers - Excellent textbook coverage of labor markets, the natural rate of unemployment, job search theory, and wage determination models.

5. Labor Economics (8th Edition) - George Borjas - McGraw-Hill - Comprehensive treatment of labor supply and demand, wage differentials, human capital investment, and unemployment theory with real-world data.

6. [Unemployment](https://www.econlib.org/library/Enc/Unemployment.html) - Concise Encyclopedia of Economics - Expert analysis of why unemployment exists, what determines its level, and the policy debates around reducing it.

7. [Civilian Unemployment Rate](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/UNRATE) - Federal Reserve Economic Data (FRED) - The official U.S. unemployment rate over time, the most-watched indicator of labor market health in the American economy.

8. [Labor Force Participation Rate](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/CIVPART) - Federal Reserve Economic Data (FRED) - Tracks the share of working-age adults who are employed or actively seeking work, revealing important trends beyond the unemployment rate.

9. [Median Usual Weekly Earnings](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/LES1252881600Q) - Federal Reserve Economic Data (FRED) - Data on what the typical American worker earns, useful for understanding how human capital and labor markets determine wages.

10. [World Development Indicators: Labor](https://data.worldbank.org/topic/labor-and-social-protection) - World Bank - International data on employment, unemployment, and labor force participation across countries for global comparison of labor markets.
